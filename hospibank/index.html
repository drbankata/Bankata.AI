Lexmedix / Hospibank

Hospital information system and EMR

The complete hospital information system and EMR, for hospitals of any size.

Hospibank by Lexmedix is the complete hospital information system and EMR for hospitals of any bed strength: admission and beds, doctors' orders and notes, nursing and the bedside medicine chart, ICU, emergency, theatre, labour room, blood bank, lab, radiology, pharmacy, billing, insurance, medical records and a full NABH 6th edition suite, on one patient record. It runs on your own server, with a standby computer that keeps a copy, and needs no internet. Its NABH 6th edition suite maps all 639 objective elements to your own records and makes the evidence pack for the assessor. There is no upper bed limit.

What Hospibank does

One program for the whole hospital

OPD, wards, ICU, emergency, theatre, labour room, blood bank, lab, radiology, pharmacy, stores, billing, insurance, medical records, support services and quality, on one patient record. Hospibank is built on Clinibank and Dxbank, so the clinic, laboratory and imaging modules come with it.

Admission, transfer, discharge and beds

Admission from OPD, emergency, day care or another hospital, with payer, package and deposit. An IP number and a barcoded wristband, a live bed board, transfers with the bed held, attendant passes, and a discharge checklist with every clearance time-stamped.

Doctors' orders, notes and results

Computerised orders for medicines, tests, imaging, procedures, diet, nursing, blood, transfer and discharge, with order sets. Verbal orders need read-back and countersign. Admission and progress notes, referrals, discharge and death summaries, and a results inbox where a critical result must be acknowledged.

Decision support that never acts by itself

Checks for allergy, duplicate medicines, interactions, dose range, kidney function, pregnancy, imaging safety, repeated tests and high-alert medicines. They are fixed rules you can read, every override needs a reason, and an alert never changes an order on its own.

The whole patient record on one screen

A header and a timeline from 26 sources, newest first, with filters and trend charts. Restricted items stay masked until someone breaks the glass with a reason, and every look is logged.

Nursing and the bedside medicine chart

Assessments, care plans and SBAR handover. Vitals with NEWS2, PEWS and MEOWS, intake and output, and the falls, pressure injury, VTE and nutrition risk scores. At the bedside the nurse scans the wristband and the medicine, and a high-alert medicine needs a second nurse.

ICU and HDU

Admission and discharge criteria, an hourly flow sheet for ventilator, infusions, GCS, RASS and lines, APACHE II, SOFA and qSOFA with their inputs shown, and care bundles with device days.

Emergency and medico-legal cases

One-screen registration with a temporary identity, five-level triage, an emergency board, emergency codes, ambulance handover and a mass-casualty mode. A medico-legal record is sealed once signed, and police copies are numbered.

Theatre, anaesthesia, recovery and CSSD

Theatre booking with a clash check, pre-anaesthesia check, consent, site marking and the WHO checklist in order. Implants, swab and instrument counts that stop on a mismatch, an anaesthesia chart, recovery scores, and CSSD that can trace a failed load to every patient it touched.

Labour room and newborn

A partograph with alert and action lines, a delivery record for each baby that writes the birth register, paired mother and baby bands, newborn examination, birth doses and NICU care.

Chemotherapy, dialysis, therapy and psychiatry on the ward

Chemotherapy cycles on the ward chart, in-patient and peritoneal dialysis, ICU kidney support, and ward therapy. Psychiatry stays are restricted to the care team, with risk assessment, observation orders, Mental Healthcare Act record sheets, restraint reports and ECT.

Blood bank

Donors, screening, collection, grouping, components, cross-match, issue and reactions, with release by a second person and a vein-to-vein trace report. Donor records are restricted.

Laboratory, radiology and NABL

The full Dxbank LIS and RIS, plus what a hospital laboratory needs for NABL: EQA scoring, measurement uncertainty, method and reference-interval checks, calibration, a competence matrix and point-of-care testing with its own QC.

Pharmacy, stores and purchase

A formulary with look-alike and sound-alike pairs and high-alert tags, dispensing to wards, crash carts, ward narcotics, and recalls traced to patients. Several stores, indents, quotations, purchase orders, goods received, consignment implants and vendor payments.

Billing, insurance and schemes

Charges come from the care, entered once. Bed days by class, packages, deposits, interim and final bills, approved discounts and GST. TPA pre-authorisation, claims, deductions and settlement with ageing, and the scheme rates and packages you use loaded as packs. A patient or a body is never held for an unpaid bill.

Medical records, coding and certificates

File completeness and deficiencies by doctor, retrieval and retention, ICD coding, birth and death registers, the medical certificate of cause of death, and a release-of-information register.

Infection control and quality indicators

Surveillance of CAUTI, CLABSI, VAP and SSI, hand hygiene, outbreaks, restricted antibiotics, incidents with root-cause analysis and a medication-error dashboard. All 32 hospital-wide and 18 department indicators of the NABH 6th edition annexure are in one register.

NABH 6th edition suite

All 639 objective elements mapped to the records that answer them. Document control, committees and minutes, audits, mock drills, internal assessment, corrective actions and a training matrix. Readiness dashboards, a mock assessment, an evidence pack and a read-only account for a visiting assessor. It gives no score and never says you comply.

A quality loop that follows up

Each indicator has an owner and a mid-month watch. When a number slips, Hospibank splits the cause by ward or shift, gives the team a list to fix now, and checks later whether the action held.

Support services

Diet lists and the kitchen sheet, linen and housekeeping with bed-clean time, assets, maintenance, calibration and medical gas, ambulance trips, the mortuary with two-person release, and home visits.

Staff, roles and reports

76 built-in role profiles, credentials and privileges, rosters, training and appraisal, a staffing calculator, two-step sign-in that works offline, management dashboards and revenue reports such as ARPOB by ward and in-patient payer mix.

Safe records

A signed note, result, dose or summary is changed only as a new version with a reason, every change is audited, and nothing clinical or financial is deleted. Psychiatry, HIV, IVF, medico-legal and VIP records open only to the care team.

Your own server, with a standby

Hospibank runs on a Windows server on your hospital network and works without the internet. A standby computer holds a copy, a downtime pack of the medicine chart, orders, beds and allergies is written every 4 hours, and the Cloud Server edition adds encrypted off-site copies.

Bring your old records

A migration kit brings in earlier visits, past discharge summaries and folders of old documents, with a trial run and undo, and keeps them out of your census, billing and NABH counts. HL7 and FHIR interfaces connect machines and other systems, and a PACS link opens the images.

Tested at 500-bed size

We loaded five years of hospital data (1.8 million patients, 3 million OPD visits) and ran 300 people working at once for 20 minutes on an ordinary 8-core laptop: 82,073 requests, no errors and no lost save. The bed and emergency boards opened in under a tenth of a second.

Linked to Callbank

Add Callbank and your phone is answered by an AI call desk that knows your OPD doctors, open slots, fees, packages and department hours, and can tell an outpatient whether a lab or radiology report is ready. Nothing about an admission, a result, a diagnosis or a bill ever leaves Hospibank.

Questions about Hospibank

Is there an upper limit on beds?

No. Hospibank is licensed by bed band, and the largest band is unlimited. We load-tested it at 500-bed size with five years of hospital data, and we size the server with you for a larger hospital.

Does it need the internet?

No. It runs on your own server on the hospital network, with a standby computer that keeps a copy. The internet is needed only for the optional cloud copies and connected services.

Does it use AI?

No. Hospibank has no AI, and its decision support is fixed rules you can read. Callbank, our AI call desk, is a separate product that you can link to it.

What happens if the server fails?

The standby computer holds a copy and can take over. A downtime pack of the medicine chart, active orders, bed list and allergies is written every 4 hours, so the wards can keep working on paper.

Does it make us NABH compliant?

No software can. Hospibank maps your records to every objective element of the 6th edition, lists the gaps and makes the evidence pack. It gives no score and never says you comply. The assessor judges.

Can we move from our current HIS?

Yes. The migration kit and the old-HIS import bring in earlier patients, visits, discharge summaries and old documents, with a preview before anything is written.

Are the clinical scores and rules ready to use?

They are starting points. Your own clinicians confirm reference ranges, scores, interaction rules and checklists before use.

What does it cost?

Hospibank is licensed by the year by bed band and modules, with no per-user fee. Price on request.

What happens when the licence ends?

You can still read, print and export everything. Care of patients already admitted keeps working for 30 days, and the emergency department can still register and treat new arrivals.
